Emergency

5

For the situations you hope you'll never need — an arrest, a death abroad, a country turning unsafe overnight, or just needing a human on the phone right now.

24/7 distress helpline (OWRC)

Official

The Overseas Workers Resource Centre is the first call for anything urgent — a passport held by an employer, unpaid wages, unsafe working conditions, or needing a legal or medical referral right now. It covers the pre-departure and reintegration stages too, not only people already abroad.

Handles: round-the-clock contact · legal/medical referral

Steps

  1. Call, email or message OWRC or your nearest mission's emergency line as soon as the problem starts — don't wait to see if it resolves itself.
  2. Describe the situation clearly: employer, location, and what's happened (wages withheld, passport taken, unsafe housing).
  3. OWRC or the mission logs the case and connects you to the right help — legal, medical, shelter, or a labour dispute officer.
  4. Keep any evidence (contract, messages, payslips) — you'll likely be asked for it later.

Usually need

  • Passport/ID details
  • Current location and a working contact number
  • Employer or agent's name, if relevant
  • Any written proof of the issue (contract, messages, payslips)

Good to know

This is a support line, not a police line — for immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services first, then OWRC.

Arrested or detained abroad

Official

You can ask local authorities to inform the nearest Indian mission and to arrange a consular visit. The mission can't secure a release, but it will visit, share a list of local lawyers, and keep your family back home informed.

Handles: consular access · lawyer referrals · family liaison

Steps

  1. Ask the arresting authority, clearly and immediately, to notify the Indian mission — this is a right under the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations.
  2. Avoid signing anything you don't fully understand until a lawyer or consular officer has seen it.
  3. Once notified, the mission normally arranges a consular visit and shares a list of local lawyers.
  4. Ask a family member or friend to also inform the mission directly, in case the official notification is delayed.

Usually need

  • Passport number and full name
  • Name/location of the detaining authority
  • A contact for next of kin

Good to know

The mission can't get someone released or intervene in a country's legal process — it monitors welfare, ensures fair treatment, and keeps family informed.

Death abroad: certificates & mortal remains

Official

Missions can help register a death with local authorities, put together the paperwork India will accept, and coordinate sending mortal remains home. Where a family can't cover the cost, the Community Welfare Fund below can help fund the repatriation.

Handles: death certificate · repatriation · ICWF funding

Steps

  1. The local authority or hospital issues a local death certificate first — nothing else can proceed without it.
  2. Register the death with the nearest Indian mission, in person or via India's consular services portal where available.
  3. The mission attests the death certificate; once a funeral home issues embalming/health clearance, it issues a No-Objection Certificate for the remains to travel.
  4. If cost is the barrier, ask the mission about ICWF support for repatriation (see Embassy & consular help below).

Usually need

  • Deceased's passport and local death certificate
  • Informant's ID and proof of relationship
  • Embalming/health certificate, for repatriation of remains

Good to know

Repatriating a body is paperwork- and time-heavy — expect days, not hours. The mission's consular section coordinates it, not the funeral home.

Crisis advisories & evacuation

Official

When a country turns unsafe — conflict, unrest, a natural disaster — the mission posts a travel advisory and, in a full evacuation, registers and moves Indian nationals out first, as in Operation Ganga or Operation Sindhu. Checking this page and following your mission's channels is the fastest way to know what's happening.

Handles: live advisories · evacuation registration

Steps

  1. Check the MEA travel advisories page for your country as soon as tensions or a disaster start making news.
  2. Register your presence with the nearest mission if it asks — often a simple online form during a crisis — this is how you get found for evacuation flights.
  3. Follow the mission's social media/website for real-time instructions rather than general news.
  4. Keep your passport, some cash, and essential documents together and reachable.

Usually need

  • Passport details
  • Current address/location
  • A working phone number

Good to know

Evacuation operations (like Operation Ganga or Operation Sindhu) are announced during the crisis itself — there's no pre-registration list to join in advance.

↗ Travel advisories — mea.gov.in

Find the right helpline number

Official

Every mission publishes its own emergency line, and MEA keeps a directory of tele-inquiry numbers by subject in Delhi. If a card on this page doesn't have the exact number you need, start here rather than guessing at one.

Handles: helpline directory · by-subject inquiry

Steps

  1. Start at MEA's tele-inquiry directory to find the right desk for your subject (passport, OCI, grievance, etc).
  2. If it's mission-specific, check that mission's own "Contact/Consular" page for a direct local number.
  3. If nothing fits, MADAD (see Embassy & consular help below) is the fallback for anything without an obvious number.

Good to know

Save the number for your specific mission once you find it — during a crisis, mission-specific lines get through faster than the general Delhi ones.

Passport

3

Everything about the little navy booklet itself: getting a new one, replacing a lost one, or getting cleared for a police check abroad.

Passport renewal & reissue

Official

Renew an expiring passport, replace a full booklet, or update details (address, name, appearance) while living outside India. Applications for those abroad are filed through the Passport Seva system at your nearest Indian Mission, or an outsourced centre (VFS/BLS) where the mission uses one.

Handles: renewal · reissue · minors

Steps

  1. Register a new account on the Passport Seva portal for your mission — use a fresh email even if you've used the portal in India before.
  2. Fill the reissue application online, upload your documents, and pay the fee.
  3. Book an appointment — directly if your mission runs its own Passport Seva Kendra, or through the outsourced VFS/BLS centre it uses.
  4. Attend in person with the printed application and original documents; photo and biometrics are captured there.
  5. Track your Application Reference Number (ARN) online until the new passport is ready for collection or courier.

Usually need

  • Current or expired passport
  • Passport-size photo to spec
  • Proof of current overseas address (residence permit, utility bill, or visa)
  • Self-attested copies of the old passport
  • For minors: birth certificate and parental ID/consent

Good to know

Apply for reissue as soon as your passport has under a year of validity left — many countries refuse entry or visas on a passport nearing expiry.

Lost, stolen or damaged passport

Official

Report the loss to local police first, then apply to your nearest mission for a replacement or, if you need to travel home urgently and there's no time for a full reissue, an Emergency Certificate valid for a single trip to India.

Handles: police report · reissue · emergency travel

Steps

  1. File a police report (FIR) with local police first — you'll need this for everything that follows.
  2. Apply on the Passport Seva portal for a replacement, selecting "lost/damaged" as the reason.
  3. If you need to travel urgently and there's no time for a full reissue, ask the mission about an Emergency Certificate instead.
  4. Attend your appointment with the police report, an affidavit of loss, and any ID you still have.

Usually need

  • Police report / FIR copy
  • Affidavit of loss
  • Photocopy of the lost passport, if you have one
  • Passport photos and proof of address

Good to know

Tatkaal (fast-track) service usually isn't available for lost/damaged cases — budget extra time.

Police Clearance Certificate (PCC)

Official

Needed for foreign employment, long-term visas, permanent residency or citizenship applications abroad. Indians already living overseas apply through the Passport Seva portal or their mission's PCC service rather than through India's local police.

Handles: PR/visa proof · employer verification

Steps

  1. Start on the Passport Seva portal for missions and choose your country/region.
  2. Register and fill the PCC application with your passport and current address details.
  3. Submit the form and documents to your mission — online upload or in person, depending on the mission.
  4. The mission forwards it for background verification in India — this is usually the longest step.
  5. Collect the certificate in person, or request postal delivery once it's ready.

Usually need

  • Self-attested copies of your passport (bio page, last two pages, ECR/observations page)
  • Proof of current residency abroad
  • Proof of purpose — job offer, visa application, or PR application

Good to know

Apply well ahead of when you actually need it — verification often takes several weeks, longer than most other services here.

OCI & citizenship

3

For anyone who has taken, or is considering, another country's citizenship while wanting to keep ties to India.

New OCI card

Official

The Overseas Citizen of India card gives a lifelong multiple-entry visa and most of the rights of an Indian citizen (short of voting and government jobs) to those of Indian origin who have taken foreign citizenship. Applied for entirely online.

Handles: eligibility · new application · document list

Steps

  1. Check eligibility — broadly, former Indian citizens, their children/grandchildren, or foreign spouses of Indian citizens/OCI holders married 2+ years.
  2. Fill the application at ociservices.gov.in and upload documents plus a photo in the required format (square, plain background).
  3. Submit the originals in person at your jurisdictional mission/FRRO — no printouts needed, they scan the originals.
  4. Give biometrics (fingerprints and photo) at the mission — required for everyone except under-12s and over-70s.
  5. Wait roughly 30 days from acknowledgement; the mission emails you about any missing documents.
  6. Collect the registration booklet in person, or authorise someone to collect it with your passport.

Usually need

  • Valid passport (6+ months validity) and naturalisation certificate
  • Proof of Indian origin (old Indian passport, or parents'/grandparents' documents)
  • Proof of current address
  • Photograph to spec (square, min. 51×51mm, plain background)

Good to know

People with Pakistani or Bangladeshi ancestry are not eligible for OCI, regardless of other qualifying criteria.

↗ Apply — ociservices.gov.in ↗ OCI eligibility FAQ

OCI renewal / reissue

Official

An OCI card must be re-issued alongside a new passport if you were under 20 when it was first issued, and once more after you turn 50 — it isn't automatic. Miscellaneous services also cover a lost card, spelling corrections and a new-passport update.

Handles: mandatory milestones · lost card · corrections

Steps

  1. Check whether you've hit a mandatory milestone — a new passport issued before age 20, or turning 50 — either one triggers a required OCI reissue.
  2. Log in to ociservices.gov.in and select the miscellaneous services / reissue option.
  3. Upload your new passport details and any changed information.
  4. Submit at your mission along with the old OCI card for updating.

Usually need

  • Current OCI card
  • New passport and passport photo
  • Proof of the triggering event (new passport bio page)

Good to know

This isn't automatic — many people only find out they needed to do this when it causes a problem at Indian immigration.

↗ OCI miscellaneous services FAQ

Surrender passport & renunciation certificate

Official

Indian law requires surrendering your Indian passport once you take another country's citizenship. Doing this promptly at your mission — often alongside an OCI application in the same visit — avoids penalties if you're later found still holding it.

Handles: surrender · renunciation certificate

Steps

  1. Fill the Surrender of Indian Passport Certificate form (or the "without passport" declaration plus a loss affidavit if you no longer have it).
  2. Gather proof of your new foreign citizenship.
  3. Submit to your mission, often via VFS/BLS, ideally in the same visit as an OCI application.
  4. Wait roughly 1–3 weeks for processing; the passport is stamped "cancelled due to acquiring foreign nationality."

Usually need

  • Original Indian passport (or a loss affidavit if you no longer have it)
  • Proof of foreign citizenship
  • Passport photos
  • For minors: parental consent form

Good to know

Indian law requires this — holding onto an Indian passport after taking another citizenship can create real complications later, even if you never intend to use it.

Embassy & consular help

4

The standing channels for ongoing problems and formal support — as distinct from the acute, right-now situations above.

Find your High Commission / Consulate

Official

The Ministry of External Affairs maintains the master directory of every Indian embassy, high commission and consulate abroad, with addresses, phone lines and jurisdiction maps for each.

Handles: address · phone · jurisdiction

Steps

  1. Go to the MEA missions directory and search by country or city.
  2. Note both your nearest mission and its specific jurisdiction — some countries have multiple consulates, each covering different regions.
  3. Save its direct contact page, not just the general mea.gov.in number.

Good to know

Bookmark this before you need it — searching for it during an actual emergency wastes time.

↗ mea.gov.in — Indian Missions Abroad ↗ Government directory listing (igod.gov.in)

MADAD — file a consular complaint

Official

MADAD is the MEA's online grievance-tracking system for overseas Indians: passport delays, mission non-response, employer disputes and more, logged and tracked to resolution instead of sent into a general inbox.

Handles: grievance tracking · status updates

Steps

  1. Register at the MADAD portal with your name, date of birth, mobile number and email.
  2. Verify your account via the confirmation link emailed to you.
  3. File your grievance — for yourself or someone else — choosing the closest matching category (imprisonment, unpaid wages, mortal remains, and so on).
  4. Save the reference number you're given and track status online.

Usually need

  • Basic ID details
  • A clear description of the issue
  • Any supporting documents (contract, correspondence)

Good to know

Cases are colour-coded by how long they've been open and escalate automatically up the chain — a MADAD complaint tends to get more traction than an email to a generic inbox.

↗ File on MADAD via mea.gov.in

Indian Community Welfare Fund (ICWF)

Official

Emergency, need-based assistance run through missions: repatriating stranded or destitute workers, funding return of a body, legal aid, and shelter for women in distress — not a loan scheme, a genuine safety net for those with nowhere else to turn.

Handles: repatriation · legal aid · shelter

Steps

  1. Contact your nearest mission directly — there's no separate online application; a consular officer assesses each case individually.
  2. Explain the emergency clearly: what's happened and what kind of help you need (lodging, airfare home, legal aid, medical care, repatriation).
  3. An officer at the mission assesses eligibility and, if approved, arranges the specific assistance.

Usually need

  • Proof you entered the country legally (passport/visa)
  • Whatever documents relate to the emergency itself

Good to know

OCI and PIO cardholders generally aren't eligible for individual ICWF assistance — it's specifically for Indian citizens/passport holders in distress.

↗ ICWF details — mea.gov.in

Pravasi Bharatiya Sahayata Kendra

Official

Walk-in help desks at missions in several Gulf countries for workers in distress — unpaid salaries, passport confiscation by an employer, or exploitative contracts — staffed for exactly this kind of case.

Handles: labour disputes · in-person help desks

Steps

  1. Locate the nearest Kendra via the mission page — these exist mainly in Gulf countries with large numbers of Indian workers.
  2. Walk in during opening hours, or call ahead if the situation is urgent.
  3. Describe the labour dispute or distress situation — passport confiscation, unpaid wages, an exploitative contract.

Usually need

  • Passport copy, if you have one
  • Any contract or correspondence with your employer

Good to know

These desks exist because many workers can't easily travel to a full mission — check whether one covers your city before assuming you need to go to the capital.

↗ Kendra locations — mea.gov.in

Documents & certification

2

Getting Indian paperwork recognised abroad, and staying registered for the certificates that keep pensions and family records current.

Apostille & attestation

Official

Degrees, marriage certificates and other Indian documents usually need an MEA apostille (for Hague Convention countries) or full attestation before a foreign government, employer or university will accept them.

Handles: education · personal · commercial docs

Steps

  1. Get the document attested at the right state-level authority first — the HRD department for education certificates, Home Department/GAD for personal certificates (birth, marriage, death), or the Chamber of Commerce for commercial documents.
  2. Once state attestation is done, submit to an MEA-outsourced collection centre — MEA no longer takes walk-ins directly for most document types.
  3. MEA affixes the apostille sticker and returns the document through the same centre.

Usually need

  • Original document plus a photocopy
  • Passport copy and proof of address
  • The state-attestation certificate from step 1

Good to know

An apostille only works for countries in the Hague Apostille Convention — for a non-member country you need full embassy attestation instead, a separate and longer process.

↗ Apostille process — mea.gov.in

Life certificate for pensioners

Official

NRI pensioners must submit an annual life certificate to keep an Indian pension running. It can be digitally verified through Jeevan Pramaan, or attested in person at a mission where digital verification isn't available.

Handles: annual renewal · digital or in-person

Steps

  1. Simplest route: submit a Digital Life Certificate via Jeevan Pramaan using your Aadhaar number and registered mobile — no travel needed if this works for you.
  2. If your pension-disbursing bank is on the RBI's approved list, its local branch or a bank officer abroad can sign the certificate instead.
  3. Otherwise, get it signed by a notary, magistrate, banker, or Indian mission official, then send it to your pension-disbursing bank/authority in India yourself or through an authorised agent.

Usually need

  • Aadhaar number linked to your pension account, and registered mobile number
  • Or your Pension Payment Order (PPO) details, for the offline route

Good to know

This has to be done every year — missing it can pause your pension until it's submitted.

↗ Jeevan Pramaan — digital life certificate

Voting rights

1

Indian citizens abroad keep their vote — it just has to be registered from outside the country.

Register as an overseas elector

Official

Indian citizens who haven't taken another country's citizenship can register to vote from their home constituency using Form 6A on the Election Commission's portal — you still have to be physically present in India to cast the ballot.

Handles: Form 6A · constituency mapping

Steps

  1. Confirm you qualify: Indian citizen, 18 or older, and haven't taken another country's citizenship.
  2. Get Form 6A from the Election Commission's site.
  3. Fill in your passport details and the Indian address that determines your voting constituency.
  4. Submit online via NVSP with scanned documents, or send/hand it to your nearest mission.
  5. Once approved, you're added to the electoral roll for that constituency — you still vote in person at that specific polling station in India.

Usually need

  • Passport-size photo
  • Self-attested passport pages (photo/detail page plus a valid visa page)

Good to know

There's no postal or online voting for NRIs yet — registration only gets you onto the roll; casting the vote still means being physically present in India on polling day.

↗ Register — nvsp.in ↗ Election Commission of India

Work & emigration

1

A step that surprises a lot of first-time workers heading to certain countries.

Emigration clearance (ECR)

Official

Passport holders in the "Emigration Check Required" category need clearance before taking up work in a set list of countries, mostly in the Gulf. It's arranged online — no need to visit an emigration office in person.

Handles: ECR check · e-Migrate registration

Steps

  1. Register a new account on emigrate.gov.in.
  2. Buy Pravasi Bharatiya Bima Yojana (PBBY) insurance from an authorised insurer first — clearance can't be completed without a policy number.
  3. Fill in job, employer and destination-country details exactly as they appear in your contract.
  4. Upload your passport, visa, and signed employment contract.
  5. Pay the processing fee online and wait for the Protector of Emigrants to review it.
  6. Download your e-Sticker once approved — this is your emigration clearance.

Usually need

  • Passport scan and passport photo
  • Visa copy and signed employment contract
  • PBBY insurance policy number

Good to know

This only applies if your passport is marked ECR — check the back pages of your passport if you're not sure which category you're in.

↗ e-Migrate — emigrate.gov.in

Tax & banking

1

The paperwork that keeps Indian bank accounts and tax filings compliant once you're a non-resident.

PAN, tax filing & NRE/NRO basics

Official

NRIs are generally exempt from mandatory PAN–Aadhaar linking, but rules have specific conditions worth checking directly. Indian income above the taxable threshold still needs a return filed, and savings held in India should sit in NRE/NRO accounts rather than a resident account.

Handles: PAN status · e-filing · FEMA banking rules

Steps

  1. Confirm your NRI/residential status for the tax year — this determines what income is taxable in India and whether PAN–Aadhaar linking applies to you.
  2. If you have taxable Indian income, log in at the Income Tax e-filing portal with your PAN and file the applicable ITR form before the deadline.
  3. Open an NRE account (for foreign earnings, fully repatriable) and/or an NRO account (for Indian-sourced income) with a bank offering NRI banking — most accept video KYC, some still require a branch visit.
  4. Convert any existing resident savings account to NRO once your status changes — holding a regular resident account as an NRI isn't compliant.

Usually need

  • PAN and passport
  • Overseas address proof and visa/residence permit copy
  • Passport photos
  • For tax filing: income details and any foreign tax paid, for DTAA relief

Good to know

Rules on PAN–Aadhaar linking and taxable thresholds change fairly often — check the e-filing portal directly rather than going by an old article.
